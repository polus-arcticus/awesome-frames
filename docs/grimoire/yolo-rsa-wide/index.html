YoloRSA: wide tiers

The wide-modulus sibling of YoloRSA: moduli spanning two EVM words (up to 512 bits) instead of one, sized against a real GNFS-based cost model rather than "small enough for a naive script."

:::info This used to be a separate top-level module ("LightningRSA") It moved here once it became clear the thing distinguishing it from YoloRSA was never deployability (both are equally deployable) but modulus width — and once the LightningRSA name was needed for an actual production-shaped recipe built on real PKCS#1 v1.5 padding, a scheme unpadded textbook RSA never graduates to regardless of modulus size. :::

Estimating a target: GNFS, not brute force​

RSA factoring isn't brute-force keyspace search — that's what "how long to crack AES-256" articles use (2^n divided by an operations/sec rate). Factoring uses the General Number Field Sieve, whose cost is sub-exponential:

L(n) = exp(1.923 · (ln n)^(1/3) · (ln ln n)^(2/3))

(1.923 = (64/9)^(1/3), the standard GNFS constant — the same formula behind NIST/ECRYPT key-length recommendation tables.) Getting an absolute time, not just relative difficulty, needs one real, documented calibration point: RSA-768's published factoring effort (Kleinjung et al., 2010) — ~2000 physical-core-years on ~2.2 GHz Opteron-class cores, 2009 hardware.

The stated attacker model​

Rather than a nation-state cluster (the RSA-768 effort itself) or an unspecified "determined solo attacker," these tiers are calibrated against a concrete, checkable hardware assumption: one Ethereum-validator-spec machine, per ethereum.org's own CPU guidance (4-core minimum, 8-core recommended), starting the instant a spend hits the mempool — "their latent compute" being put to use the moment there's something worth cracking.

The genuinely hand-wavy part — stated explicitly rather than hidden — is the combined per-core speedup from a 2009 Opteron to a modern validator-grade core, including modern NFS software (CADO-NFS) being better-optimized than 2009-era code: 8 cores, 10x combined speedup (80 "2009-core-equivalents"), the middle of a 3x-30x sensitivity range checked before committing to a number.

The resulting ladder​

Target durationbitsstatus
10 minutes~384ten-minute
1 hour~424one-hour
1 day~502fits the 2-word template, not yet built
1 week~553needs a 3rd word
1 month~593needs a 3rd word

The 2-word ceiling (512 bits) covers ten minutes through about a day at this hardware assumption — a week or a month would need a third word, a natural extension of the same template.

Design consequence: the message-representative shortcut simplifies further​

msgHash (compute_sig_hash(tx)) is always exactly one word (< 2²⁵⁶). Once n > 2²⁵⁶ — i.e. its high word is nonzero — msgHash mod n is msgHash itself; no reduction ever actually happens. YoloRSAWideAccount.yul's constructor enforces a nonzero high word for exactly this reason: it's what lets the verify function skip implementing genuine 512-bit modular reduction and just check that MODEXP's 64-byte result has a zero high word and msgHash as its low word. Deploying with a modulus that doesn't actually need the second word would silently break that shortcut — use YoloRSA for anything that small.

Layout​

  • contracts/src/grimoire/YoloRSA/YoloRSAWideAccount.yul — the 2-word account template. Storage slot 0 = e, slots 1-2 = n's high/low words; constructor takes e, nHigh, nLow as three right-aligned words (96 bytes) appended after initcode.
  • contracts/test/js/utils/bigWord.ts — generic big-endian word split/join (splitWords/joinWords).
  • contracts/scripts/gen-yolo-rsa-wide-vectors.ts → contracts/test/vectors/yolo-rsa-wide-vectors.json.
  • contracts/test/js/YoloRSAWide.test.ts — pinned vectors, wire-encoding round-trips, and a live sign/verify check at a size distinct from every pinned tier.
