MlDsa — conceptual, not implemented

Real parameters, no working sign() or verify(). This page — and the file it documents — exist to record why on purpose, rather than ship a half-working implementation. "No half-finished implementations" is a rule this whole codebase follows; this is what following it looks like when a scheme turns out to be bigger than its survey slot.

Why ML-DSA stopped here, next to MlKem going all the way​

Both are Module-Lattice schemes over the same ring dimension (n=256), and both were candidates for this survey's one full-build lattice slot. MlKem won it specifically because it needed nothing beyond a matrix of noisy linear equations (Module-LWE) and a straightforward NTT. ML-DSA needs everything MlKem needs, plus a second layer MlKem never touches:

  • Fiat-Shamir with Aborts (Lyubashevsky's technique) — unlike MlKem's encryption, which just tolerates its own noise, ML-DSA's signing loop must reject and retry whenever a candidate signature's coefficients get too large or leak information about the secret key. A real, bounded-but-genuine retry loop, not a single deterministic pass.
  • High/low bit decomposition (Decompose, HighBits, LowBits) — splitting each coefficient of the noisy product into a coarse "high" part (what gets signed) and a "low" part that's also a rejection-sampling gate in the same step. MlKem's Compress/Decompress are lossy rounding for bandwidth; ML-DSA's decomposition is that and a validity check.
  • Hint bits — a compact, side-channel-safe way to tell the verifier which coefficients need help reconstructing the correct high bits after the low bits were discarded. MlKem's ciphertext has no analog of this structure at all.

None of that is individually exotic — each has a clean FIPS 204 spec — but stacking three extra, genuinely stateful/branchy mechanisms on an already-real NTT ring is a meaningfully bigger lift than MlKem's, and deserves the same primary-source-derivation rigor (byte-for-byte against @noble/post-quantum's ml_dsa44, the way MlKem and Falcon both got) rather than a rushed partial attempt this pass.

Real parameters, confirmed not guessed​

{n: 256, q: 8_380_417, k: 4, l: 4, d: 13, gamma1: 131_072, gamma2: 95_232, tau: 39, eta: 2, omega: 80}

Confirmed against @noble/post-quantum's own PARAMS and ml_dsa44.lengths (publicKey: 1312 bytes, secretKey: 2560 bytes, signature: 2420 bytes) — not from memory.

What a future full pass would need​

Derive KeyGen/Sign/Verify from the FIPS 204 PDF directly — the same primary-source discipline MlKem's derivation needed, given how easy the Decompose/MakeHint/UseHint boundary conditions are to get subtly wrong — then cross-check byte-for-byte against ml_dsa44 the way MlKem's generator does. One open question worth resolving early: because Sign's rejection behavior is genuinely random-length, byte-exact matching may not be achievable for Sign without also reimplementing @noble/post-quantum's exact rejection-retry RNG consumption — landing on Verify-only, the scope Falcon settled into for a different reason (floating point), might turn out to be the honest answer here too.
